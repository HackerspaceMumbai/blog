# Dev Days Mumbai 2026: More Than an Event, A Community in Motion

On September 5, 2026, developers from across Mumbai came together at Paytm's Andheri East office for **Dev Days Mumbai** - a community-led exploration of AI-powered software development, GitHub Copilot, open source, and modern engineering workflows. What emerged was more than a conference. It was a day of learning, building, teaching, and connecting.

<Image src={HeroWideImage} alt="GitHub Copilot Dev Days Mumbai - community gathering at Paytm Office, Andheri East" />

## Building the Right Room

Dev Days Mumbai drew more than **500 registrations**. Venue capacity meant attendance was curated on purpose: students, early-career developers, experienced engineers, architects, open source contributors, startup builders, AI enthusiasts, and community leaders sharing one room.

The goal was never simply to fill seats. It was to make room for conversations between people with different experiences and a shared appetite for learning - hallway chats included.

## GitHub as a Social Network for Developers

People often describe GitHub as a code hosting platform. That understates what it has become: the world's largest social network for developers, where ideas move through repositories, pull requests, discussions, and contributions.

That philosophy shaped Dev Days Mumbai. Speakers anchored sessions in repositories, real code, and practical implementation - not one-off slide decks. Attendees discovered resources, explored projects, and kept the conversation going after the room emptied.


<Image src={CollaborativeWorkshop} alt="Attendees collaborating on laptops during a workshop at Dev Days Mumbai" />

Community is not built when people only sit in rows and watch. It is built when people collaborate.

## A Day of Practical Engineering

The agenda focused on real-world implementation as AI becomes part of everyday development workflows. The day opened with a keynote by [Augustine Correa](https://stars.github.com/profiles/indcoder) on the future of AI-powered software development and agentic workflows, before diving into a series of practical sessions covering GitHub Copilot, cloud-native engineering, security, and production-ready AI tooling.

<Image src={StrongStageSpeaker} alt="Speaker presenting on stage at Dev Days Mumbai" />

### Multi-Agent Orchestration with Omnigent

**Anas Khan** explored multi-agent systems - orchestration patterns, tooling, and practical techniques for coordinating AI-assisted workflows.

### Closed-Loop Architectures: Making GitHub Copilot Prompt Itself

**Pratik Pathak** showed how compiler diagnostics, test results, and runtime feedback can feed AI-assisted development, with a practical look at self-improving feedback loops.

### Gateway-Level Caching & High-Scale Systems

**Anshul Bansal** unpacked caching strategies, performance bottlenecks, and architectural trade-offs from operating systems at scale.

### Copilot in the Classroom: Adopt Fast, Govern Faster

**Alham Shakeel Ahmed Ansari** addressed a question many organizations face: how to adopt AI quickly while keeping governance and responsibility intact.

### Scaling AI Initiatives

**Paytm's AI team** shared how enterprise AI moves from experimentation to operationalization - and the organizational challenges of scaling across a large technology company. Their contribution went beyond hosting: they gave attendees a view into AI applied and governed at industry scale.

## Learning by Building

Talks sparked ideas; the workshop turned them into practice. More than **40 developers** worked in pairs - one with the GitHub Copilot App, another with the Copilot CLI - comparing approaches on real tasks.

Mentors including **Pankaj Gupta**, **Pratik Pathak**, and **Anas Khan** helped participants through prompts, agents, automation, and debugging. Peer-to-peer exploration remained one of the clearest lessons of the day.

## Celebrating the Teachers Among Us

Dev Days Mumbai fell on Teacher's Day. Teaching does not only happen in classrooms - it happens through open source, pull request reviews, blogs, docs, talks, mentorship, workshops, and community events.

Every speaker, mentor, volunteer, and attendee who shared an insight sustained that culture. The ecosystem is built by people writing code. It is sustained by people sharing knowledge.

## Community Moments

Some of the strongest moments sat between the sessions: careers, open source, architecture, startups, and emerging tech.

One of them was a GitHub Star plaque that circulated through the room and into photos - less about one name on metal, more about a community that keeps learning, teaching, and building together.
